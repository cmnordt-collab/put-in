About Put-In

Put-In is a free, independently built guide to paddling access in North Carolina for SUPs, kayaks, and canoes. It exists because the information paddlers need before loading the car was scattered across dozens of agency websites, Facebook groups, and word of mouth, and nobody had pulled it together in one place.

Who built it

I am a paddler in the NC Piedmont, a lifelong swimmer and water enthusiast, and a former open water racer. I started paddling seriously when I was coaching and training for open water 5k and 10k races, and fell in love with it just as much as swimming. It became a way to explore the coast on trips and to stay on the water when summer temperatures or time constraints made open-water swimming impossible in our NC summers.

Over the years, I found myself researching water quality reports, hunting for new spots, checking conditions, and building mental maps of what was actually paddleable and what was not. I love collecting and using that kind of data, and Put-In is where that habit finally has a home.

Why it exists

Too many drives ended in a locked gate, a hire-only lake, a carry that was impossible with a hard board alone, or other conditions nobody warned about. When I was racing, I learned to take water quality seriously. Warm temperatures, algal blooms, and runoff are real issues on NC waters, and knowing who monitors a lake before I got in it mattered a lot to me. Growing up visiting the NC coast made me aware early on of what can go wrong when you are not prepared for the changing tides, submerged hazards, or weather conditions that move faster than you do.

Put-In is the resource I wished existed. Every spot is checked against its managing agency and includes its source. As a safety note, blank fields mean unverified, not absent — that distinction matters most for hazards and dams.

What it is not

Put-In is not a substitute for scouting, and it is not a safety guarantee. Conditions change faster than any database can keep up with. Always check current conditions before you go, tell someone your float plan, and paddle within your skill level.

As this site evolves, I hope to include features that help increase paddlers’ safety and expertise, route planning, and eventually expand outside of NC. If others in the paddling community wish to contribute their experiences, please contact me below!

Cold Weather Paddling

Dress for the water temperature, not the air temperature. Your body loses heat 25x faster in water than in air. NC water temps drop into the 40s and 50s from November through March on Piedmont rivers and lakes. Avoid cotton entirely — it does not insulate when wet.

NRS Cove Neoprene Paddling Gloves NRS is the brand trusted by outfitters and whitewater guides for paddling gloves. 2mm neoprene for shoulder season, 3mm for cold water. Fingers stay nimble enough to operate buckles and dry bag closures. ~$30–45 Amazon — affiliate link
